\section{\src{Discussion and conclusion}}\label{sec:discussion}

\src{Overall, approval-day information predicts interruption only weakly, and no model is reliably better. On the 28 test programmes, balanced accuracy ranges from 0.51 to 0.57 at the model-specific thresholds in Table~\ref{tab:model-comparison}, against 0.500 for the baseline. At a common threshold of 0.50, the forest (0.567), logistic regression (0.570) and network (0.572) differ by 0.005, while one additional correct prediction moves balanced accuracy by about 0.03 to 0.05. Logistic regression has the highest AUC (0.684).}

\src{Several limitations qualify these findings, beginning with the treatment of parallel and successive programmes. Same-country, same-day blends share a review schedule and are merged; overlapping and successive programmes remain separate observations, with history captured by track-record inputs. There are 13 programmes overlapping earlier ones (11 in training), and 43 of the 84 training countries have several programmes. Cancelled programmes replaced by successors count as interrupted, including some superseded arrangements. Section~\ref{sec:data-sources} describes country CV; 26 of the 28 test programmes come from training countries, so the test mainly assesses known borrowers. In addition, the test set is small (28 programmes), its programmes are larger, longer and less often concessional, scheduled reviews partly define the outcome, and the macro inputs are staff projections.}

\src{From a policy perspective, these scores could complement staff judgement rather than flag programmes independently; the mechanical role of scheduled reviews does not justify scheduling fewer reviews. The findings for extended programmes also need careful interpretation: they were interrupted less often (38\% against 60\% in training), mainly because of ECFs (36\%); EFFs (57\%) resembled SBAs (60\%). Long relationships can create moral hazard: expected waivers, modified targets or lenient reviews weaken borrower incentives, while lenders may hesitate to interrupt. The IMF’s Independent Evaluation Office examined prolonged use, and Reinsberg et al. (2022) use “dependency trap” in their title. Completed reviews cannot distinguish compliance from leniency, so high completion alone is weak evidence of success.}

\src{For further research, one avenue would be to use pre-2002 review histories from other IMF sources or completed post-2016 programmes, assessed with rolling time-ordered splits. Re-estimating the models without the number of scheduled reviews would measure the mechanical part of the signal.}
